# Writing — English

Apply this to English decks, pitches, submissions, READMEs, issues, and reports. Follow `common.md` first.

## Sentences

- Prefer short, direct sentences.
- Use active voice when the actor is known.
- Put the subject and action early in the sentence.
- Avoid unnecessary nominalizations. Prefer verbs over phrases such as "the execution of" or "the generation of."
- Do not hedge by default with "can," "may," "might," or "it seems" when the claim is verified.

## Natural English

- Do not preserve Korean word order when translating.
- Rewrite for natural English rather than translating sentence by sentence.
- Avoid filler such as "it is important to note that," "in terms of," "through this," and "in order to" when a direct sentence works.
- Prefer common technical verbs such as "run," "merge," "generate," "verify," and "deploy" over inflated alternatives.
- Keep canonical product names and standard technical terms in English.

## Tone

- Use plain technical English.
- Avoid marketing adjectives unless evidence supports them.
- Avoid consultant-style abstractions when a concrete product, action, or result can be named.
- Keep headings short and informative.

## Pitch

- Write for speech, not for a report.
- Use one idea per sentence.
- Prefer concrete transitions tied to the demo: "Now we merge the PRs" is better than an abstract transition.
- Explain unfamiliar terms once, then use the standard term consistently.
